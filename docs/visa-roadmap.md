# Visa Roadmap

Last updated: 2026-04-02

## Purpose

This note summarizes the current working immigration strategy for building the company in Japan while preserving an eventual path toward `HSP(i)(b)` based on a CTO-led technical role.

## Current Status

- The founder is already in Japan.
- Current status is `Designated Activities (40)`.
- Current Japan residence history is approximately one year.
- The startup visa route should therefore be handled as a change-of-status path from another in-Japan status, not as a first entry from overseas.

## Core Strategy

1. Apply from inside Japan for the Tokyo startup visa route by changing from the current status to startup-related `Designated Activities`.
2. Use the startup visa period, including extensions if available, to establish the company, validate the product, and build commercial substance.
3. Maintain the formal intention of progressing to `Business Manager`, because that is the expected logic of the startup visa route.
4. Structure roles so that the cofounder is clearly the business lead and CEO, while the Japan-based founder is clearly the CTO focused on applied cryptography, MPC wallet infrastructure, security architecture, and implementation.
5. If, by the end of the startup visa period, the company is still too early for the current `Business Manager` requirements, pursue a separate `HSP(i)(b)` application based on the CTO's primary technical role under contract with the Japan company.

## Working Timeline

### Phase 1: Startup Visa Entry

Target:
- Obtain TMG startup-plan confirmation and then change status inside Japan into startup-related `Designated Activities`.

Main workstreams:
- Complete the startup package in `docs-visa/1_municipality_screening_package`
- Prepare the startup plan, founder resume, funds proof, residence proof, and qualification evidence
- Keep the application story consistent with a real startup being formed in Japan
- Prepare the additional change-of-status materials required for in-Japan filing, including the explanation letter for changing from the current status and current residence-card materials

Key message:
- The company is being established in Japan, with the cofounder leading sales and operations and the Japan-based founder leading technical development as CTO.

### Phase 2: Operate During Startup Visa Period

Target:
- Build factual support for the company's technical and commercial substance during the initial 12 months.

Main workstreams:
- Incorporate the company
- Maintain a Japan address and operating base
- Build the wallet and escrow product
- Produce technical outputs: architecture docs, source code, audits, prototypes, demos, integration materials
- Build customer pipeline and early revenue evidence if possible

Role discipline:
- Cofounder should remain the obvious business operator and CEO
- CTO should remain the obvious technical specialist

### Phase 3: Startup Visa Extensions If Needed

Target:
- If necessary, extend the startup visa in six-month increments, up to the currently stated Tokyo maximum of two years

Reason:
- Additional time may be needed to complete company setup, product buildout, and commercial validation before a status change

Operational requirement:
- Renewal materials should continue to show real progress against the startup preparation activity plan
- The formal story remains that the business is moving toward meeting `Business Manager` requirements.

### Phase 4: Evaluate Change to `HSP(i)(b)`

Target:
- If facts support it, file a separate change-of-status application to `HSP(i)(b)` if the company is still not in position to satisfy the current `Business Manager` threshold

This route is strongest if:
- the Japan company is the contracting organization
- the applicant has a formal CTO or equivalent technical contract with that company
- day-to-day work is highly specialized technical work
- management authority is clearly centered on the CEO/cofounder
- the evidence package is technical rather than managerial

Technical narrative:
- applied cryptography
- MPC wallet infrastructure
- key management and wallet security
- smart-contract and escrow implementation
- backend systems and developer tooling

### Phase 5: Consider `Business Manager` Later

Target:
- Revisit `Business Manager` once the company has stronger revenue, more operational substance, and a better reason to use that status

Why this is deferred:
- the current `Business Manager` route appears materially more burdensome than older summaries suggested, including the currently published requirement of `JPY 30M` in business assets plus at least `1` full-time employee
- if the founder's real activity remains technical, `HSP(i)(b)` may fit better than early business-management status

## Why `HSP(i)(b)` Is Being Pursued

The current reasoning is:
- the Japan-based founder's strongest profile is technical, not sales or general management
- the startup is a software infrastructure business
- the founder's intended activity is coding and technical architecture, not day-to-day business operations
- the cofounder is the better fit for CEO, sales, partnerships, and operations

The intended filing position is not:
- "software company therefore HSP(i)(b)"

The intended filing position is:
- "the applicant's primary activity in Japan is highly specialized technical work under contract with a Japan company, while the cofounder handles business leadership"

## Non-Negotiable Structuring Assumptions

To keep the `HSP(i)(b)` route credible, the following assumptions should stay true in practice:

1. Cofounder is CEO and business lead.
2. Japan-based founder is CTO and technical lead.
3. The Japan-based founder is not the main person for sales, fundraising, operations, or general management.
4. A formal technical employment agreement or equivalent contract exists with the Japan company.
5. The evidence trail shows technical output and technical supervision, not mainly managerial activity.

If those assumptions break down, the `HSP(i)(b)` theory weakens.

## Main Risks

1. Startup visa logic in Tokyo is still oriented toward future `Business Manager` qualification.
2. Immigration may look past titles and focus on the substance of the founder's real activity.
3. A 50/50 ownership structure does not by itself block `HSP(i)(b)`, but it increases scrutiny if the founder appears to be effectively managing the business.
4. If the founder becomes the practical decision-maker for business operations, the case can drift toward `Business Manager` or `HSP(i)(c)` instead.
5. The current `Designated Activities (40)` background may affect procedural details depending on the exact basis of that status, so the change-of-status filing mechanics should be checked carefully before submission.

## Two-Track Summary

Track 1:
- Startup visa for up to two years, pursued on the formal basis that the company is preparing to qualify for `Business Manager`.

Track 2:
- If the business is still not ready to meet the current `Business Manager` requirements by the end of the startup visa period, meaning `JPY 30M` in business assets plus at least `1` full-time employee, submit a separate `HSP(i)(b)` application centered on the CTO's technical activity.
